Our games

One start. One target. And the way between.

Every level shows you where it starts and where it should end. In DREH and ZEHN you play cards to get there, each only as often as it allows. In WERK you build a machine, in GRIFF you program robot arms, in STROM you wire up a circuit, in GLEIS you lay track for trains that run to a timetable, in SPIEGEL you bend light with mirrors, in SCHATTEN you build a sculpture whose shadows have to fit exactly, in GANG you set gears until every dial turns just right, in KIPP you tilt a board until every marble lies in its hole, and in WÜRFEL you roll a cube until every colour is stamped where it belongs. How you get there is the whole puzzle.

Word puzzle

DREH DREH

“One word. One target. Order decides.”

Turn a starting word into the target word, one move at a time – with cards like “reverse”, “drop last” or “W first”. That is how a WORM becomes a COW and SANE becomes a SWAN.

  • 16Worlds
  • 640Levels per language
  • 11Card types
  • 2Play modes
  • Sixteen themed worlds of 40 levels each – from Animals, Nature and Kitchen to Fantasy and Space.
  • Separate level catalogues in English and in German.
  • Easy: every move happens at once. Hard: you plan all your moves first, then run the plan.
  • Every level is proven solvable by the solver, and the shortest solution is known – can you match it?
  • Twelve designs and six music tracks, unlocked with coins you earn by solving levels.
  • Coming soon to Google Play
  • Coming soon to the App Store

Number puzzle

ZEHN ZEHN

“One number. One target. Order decides.”

DREH’s twin, with numbers instead of words: a starting number, a target number, a handful of maths cards – and real maths along the way.

  • 24Worlds
  • 1,200Levels
  • 40Cards
  • 2Play modes
  • A course through maths: plus and minus, times and divide, squares and roots, powers, fractions, percentages, logarithms and trigonometry. The last five worlds mix it all.
  • 29 maths cards plus 11 cards from DREH that work on digits: 120 reversed is 21.
  • Exact maths: every number is a reduced fraction, never a rounded decimal. If a result does not work out, the card tells you why.
  • The same 1,200 levels in English and in German, with measured, steadily rising difficulty.
  • Easy or Hard, twelve designs and six music tracks – just like DREH.
  • Coming soon to Google Play
  • Coming soon to the App Store

Robot-arm puzzle

GRIFF GRIFF

“Program the arms. Press start. Watch them work.”

Stores hand out coloured tiles, outputs want particular stacks. You set up robot arms and give each one a short program of command keys. Then you press start: all arms work at once, tick by tick, repeating their programs over and over.

  • 10Halls
  • 160Levels
  • 7Commands
  • 0Time pressure
  • Ten halls, from First Grip to Master Hand with many arms in tight spaces.
  • Seven commands: turn right, turn left, grab, drop, extend, retract and wait.
  • Stacks up to three layers high, painters for the top layer, and belts in the later halls.
  • Two bests per level: as few commands and as few ticks as possible.
  • No luck, no time pressure: the arms only run when you press start, and exactly the same way every time.

  • Coming soon to the App Store

Circuit puzzle

STROM STROM

“Build the circuit. Press test. Let it shine.”

Switches on the left, lamps on the right, and in between you build the circuit from wires and gates. Then the test runs step by step: the switches change, current flows visibly through your wires, and every lamp has to light exactly when it should.

  • 10Worlds
  • 160Levels
  • 8Parts
  • 0Time pressure
  • Ten worlds, from the first Hook-up to Arithmetic.
  • Eight parts: wire, crossing, splitter, NOT, AND, XOR, delay and latch.
  • A test table shows, step by step, which lamp should light – and where your circuit is off.
  • Delays and latches bring in time: clocks, blinkers and small memories.
  • Every level has a checked reference circuit. Can you match it with as few parts?
  • Coming soon to Google Play
  • Coming soon to the App Store

Railway puzzle

GLEIS GLEIS

“Lay the track. Set the points. Every train arrives.”

Depots stand at the edge with their timetables, stations wait on the board in the trains’ colours. In between you lay the line: straights, curves, points and signals. Then you press Go, and all the trains run at once, tick by tick. Each one has to reach the station of its colour without derailing and without a crash.

  • 10Worlds
  • 160Levels
  • 7Pieces of track
  • 0Time pressure
  • Ten worlds, from the first track through points, crossings and signals to the central station with up to six trains.
  • Seven pieces of track: straight, curve, crossing, points, colour points, flip points and signal.
  • One swipe of a finger lays a whole line; the curves appear by themselves.
  • One best score per level, checked by a solver: use as little track as possible for three stars.
  • No luck, no time pressure: the trains only run when you press Go, and exactly the same way every time.

Gear puzzle

GANG GANG

“Place the gears. The motor runs. Every dial turns right.”

Between motors and dials you set gears, belts and summing gears from a box with an exact count of each. Every dial wants to turn its own way at its own speed. The gears are drawn to scale: small meshes with large, medium with medium, and a summing gear adds two speeds together.

  • 14Worlds
  • 350Levels
  • 6Parts
  • 0Time pressure
  • 14 worlds, from the first gear through belts, crossed belts and two motors to the clock tower.
  • Gears drawn to scale: small meshes with large, medium with medium, bigger pairs jam.
  • A summing gear adds two speeds: 3 + 1 = 4, and against each other they subtract.
  • A proven best score for every level: the fewest parts earn three stars.
  • No luck, no time pressure: what you set turns exactly like that, every time.

The studio

The order is the puzzle.

Riddlewerk is a small indie studio making puzzle games for phones. We build small, calm games that fit into a break and still give you a real aha moment.

They all share one idea: you see the start and the target, and you hold every card in your hand. Nothing is hidden, nothing comes down to luck. Whether you get there depends only on the order you play in.

DREH does it with words, ZEHN with numbers. In WERK you build the order yourself: a machine of belts and splitters that runs tick by tick. GRIFF turns it into programs for robot arms, STROM into a circuit that current flows through step by step, GLEIS into a railway where every train has to arrive on its timetable. SPIEGEL bends light over mirrors and prisms until every target glows in its colour. SCHATTEN goes into the third dimension: a sculpture of cubes whose shadows have to fit exactly from three sides. GANG does the sums with gears: small meshes with large, and a summing gear adds two speeds, until every dial turns exactly as fast as it should. KIPP rolls every marble at once: each tilt moves every marble, and deflectors, tubes and gates decide where it ends up. WÜRFEL rolls in 3D across several floors: which face lands where is the puzzle.

  • Every level solvable

    Every level is checked by a solver before it ships. The shortest solution is known, and the game gives you its number of moves as the target.

  • No account, no analytics

    You do not need an account. Your progress and settings stay on your device, and our games contain no analytics services.

  • Ads in moderation

    Full-screen ads only appear after a solved level, never in your first session and at most every three levels. “Ad-free” removes them completely.

  • At your own pace

    With “Reduce motion”, everything happens at once instead of animating. Music, effects and vibration can each be switched off.
